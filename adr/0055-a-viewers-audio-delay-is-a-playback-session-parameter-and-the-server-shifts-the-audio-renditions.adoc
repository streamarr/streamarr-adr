= ADR 0055: A Viewer's Audio Delay Is a Playback Session Parameter, and the Server Shifts the Audio Renditions by It

== Status

Proposed

This decision extends link:0043-audio-tracks-and-text-subtitles-are-hls-renditions.adoc[ADR 0043].
Every audio rendition of a stream session plays shifted by the playback session's audio delay.
This decision amends link:0037-worker-delivers-fmp4-segments-from-ffmpeg-stdout.adoc[ADR 0037].
A job attempt in a playback session with a positive audio delay delivers an audio lead, which the producer otherwise discards as preroll.
This decision changes link:0045-clients-direct-play-covered-files-and-use-platform-players.adoc[ADR 0045] and link:0052-a-failed-hls-copy-falls-back-to-an-encode-and-original-changes-to-auto.adoc[ADR 0052].
With an audio delay other than 0, Original plays the HLS copy instead of direct play whenever one capability entry accepts the HLS copy.
This decision depends on three audio rules that pending amendments to ADR 0037 and ADR 0043 add (https://github.com/streamarr/streamarr-adr/pull/84[streamarr/streamarr-adr#84], https://github.com/streamarr/streamarr-adr/pull/85[streamarr/streamarr-adr#85]).
<<decision-prerequisites>> restates them, and this decision holds them as requirements whatever form those amendments take.

== Context

Lip-sync error is the offset between the sound a viewer hears and the picture the viewer sees.
Most of it comes from the viewer's playback chain, not from the file.
A TV's picture processing delays the picture.
A soundbar or a receiver delays the sound while it decodes and processes it.
Bluetooth headphones and AirPlay delay the sound too.
Apple's documentation of `AVAudioSession.outputLatency` says that an AirPlay device can add about 2 s.
The viewer's chain differs from one device and audio output to the next, so one correct offset exists per device and per output.

Streamarr's own contribution to the offset is small.
With the pending amendments, Streamarr holds its own offset within ±5 ms after a start, a seek, a rendition switch and a quality switch.
ITU-R BT.1359 puts the detectability threshold at 45 ms with the sound early and 125 ms with the sound late.
EBU R37 asks a broadcast chain to stay within 40 ms early and 60 ms late.
So a viewer whose chain adds more than about 45 ms of skew notices it, and Streamarr cannot remove that skew without the viewer's help.

No platform player that Streamarr uses offers an audio delay for HLS:

* `AVPlayerItem` has no audio offset, and `AVAudioSession.outputLatency` is read-only.
* Apple's What's new in HLS 2026 (June 2026, page 5) lets an `MTAudioProcessingTap` process the mixed audio of an HLS item, except FairPlay-protected audio.
A tap processes samples as they play, so it can delay the sound but cannot play it earlier.
* `HTMLMediaElement` has no audio offset, and hls.js 1.7.3 has no setting for one.
A Web Audio `DelayNode` can delay the sound only, and it mixes to the destination's channel count.

Two Plex clients offer an audio delay: Rivulet and plezy.
Rivulet's engine, AetherEngine, re-times the segments that it writes for AVPlayer and replaces the player item at the current position to apply a change.
AetherEngine measured about 0.3 s of held picture per change, with the position kept to the sample.
Swiftfin, a Jellyfin client, offers a delay only on its VLCKit player and resets it for every title.

The client tickets https://github.com/streamarr/streamarr-web/issues/137[streamarr/streamarr-web#137] and https://github.com/streamarr/streamarr-apple/issues/127[streamarr/streamarr-apple#127] add an audio delay setting to the web and Apple players.
Both offer ±2,000 ms in 10 ms steps.
Both recommend that the server deliver the audio renditions shifted by the delay, and both wait for this decision.

Under ADR 0043, every audio track is its own audio rendition, on the stream session's segment list.
The server keeps the first delivery of each audio segment.
Under ADR 0037, every delivered media segment is one `moof` and one `mdat`, and the `trun` gives each sample its own duration, size and flags.
That layout is part of the worker contract (link:0054-workers-measure-each-stream-bitrate-and-a-copy-advertises-its-measured-peak.adoc#amends-0037[ADR 0054]).

== Decision

[[decision-parameter]]
=== The audio delay is a parameter of the playback session

The audio delay is a signed number of milliseconds.
A positive delay plays the sound later than the picture, and a negative delay plays it earlier.
The delay runs from −2,000 ms to +2,000 ms in steps of 10 ms, and it is 0 by default.

The audio delay belongs to the playback session.
Every stream session of the playback session uses it, including the stream session of each new format attempt (ADR 0052).
The client sends the delay when it creates the playback session, as `audioDelayMilliseconds` in the streaming options of `createPlaybackSession`.
The client changes the delay during playback with the mutation `setPlaybackSessionAudioDelay`, which takes the playback session's id and the new delay.
The mutation returns the playback session, whose ready format attempt carries a `streamUrl` for the new delay.
The server rejects a delay outside the range, or one that is not a multiple of 10 ms, with the typed error `InvalidAudioDelayError` (link:0026-mutation-payloads-and-error-channels.adoc[ADR 0026]).
Each client clamps the value to the range before it sends it.

The server keeps the delay only while the playback session lasts.
The server never stores the delay with the Account, the Profile, the device registration or the watch state.

*Why:* The delay describes a device's playback chain, not a title and not a person.
The same Profile can watch through a receiver on one evening and through AirPods on the next.
2 s covers the worst case that Apple names for AirPlay, and AetherEngine clamps at the same value.
10 ms is under a quarter of the smallest offset that BT.1359 says a viewer detects.
A finer step would add presses on a remote without an audible benefit.
A playback session already outlives its replacement stream sessions, so a delay stored there survives format recovery without new rules.

[[decision-client-storage]]
=== Each client keeps the delay per device and per audio output

Each client stores the delay on the device and sends it with every playback session.
The Apple client keys the value by the current output port's `uid` from `AVAudioSession.currentRoute`.
A route without a stored value uses 0.
When the route changes during playback, the Apple client sends the new route's value with `setPlaybackSessionAudioDelay`.
The web client stores the value in `localStorage` for the browser profile, inside a `try`/`catch`, and uses 0 when storage is unavailable.
Where the browser exposes `HTMLMediaElement.setSinkId()`, the web client keeps one value per audio output device id.
A client added later, such as the Android TV client, keys the value by its platform's audio output in the same way.
The delay never travels with the Account and never reaches another device.

*Why:* HDMI to a receiver and AirPods on the same Apple TV need different values.
A value synced to the Account would apply one chain's correction to another chain.

[[decision-shift]]
=== The server shifts every audio rendition by moving timestamps, never samples

The server stores each audio rendition's segments as the worker delivers them, without the delay.
To serve an audio segment for a delay, the server builds the segment from the stored segments.
It adds the delay to the start of every stored sample.
It places each sample in the segment whose interval holds the sample's shifted start, the same placement rule that ADR 0037 and ADR 0043 apply to every audio sample.
It writes the segment as one `moof` and one `mdat` in ADR 0037's layout, with the shifted decode time in the `tfdt`.
Each sample keeps its bytes, size, duration and flags.

The shift is the whole number of the track's timescale ticks nearest to the delay.
The timescale of an audio rendition is its sample rate.
Every 10 ms step is then a whole number of ticks at any rate that is a multiple of 100 Hz, such as 44.1, 48, 96 and 192 kHz.
At 11.025 and 22.05 kHz, the shift differs from the delay by at most half a tick, which is 45 µs.

The shift applies to copied and encoded audio alike.
This is how a delay applies to a copy: the copy's timestamps move, and its samples do not.
A copy is never resampled, re-encoded or cut inside a frame for the delay, so its packets stay bit-identical.
A sample whose shifted start falls before media time zero is dropped, as ADR 0043 drops audio before media time zero.
The last segment takes every sample after its interval, as in ADR 0037.

The delay changes nothing else in the stream session:

* Each audio rendition's initialization segment stays the same for every delay.
* The segment list, the `EXTINF` durations, the variants' video segments and the WebVTT subtitle segments stay the same.
Subtitle cues stay on the picture's time.
* Every audio rendition of the stream session uses the same delay, so a rendition switch keeps it.
* The stream decision ignores the delay.
The delay never changes which tracks the server copies, the session's audio codec, the audio step or the cost of a video copy (link:0050-viewer-limits-bind-copies-and-quality-tiers-are-picture-boxes.adoc[ADR 0050], ADR 0054).

The multivariant playlist's `BANDWIDTH` counts each audio rendition's peak over the shifted intervals.
For a copied audio rendition, the server bounds a shifted segment's audio by the keyframe intervals that overlap the segment's interval moved back by the delay, widened by one audio frame on each side as ADR 0054 does.

A shifted segment uses audio from two stored segments.
With a positive delay, segment `N` takes the end of stored segment `N − 1`, or the audio lead when the server holds no segment `N − 1` (<<decision-audio-lead>>).
With a negative delay, segment `N` takes the start of stored segment `N + 1`.
The server holds a request for a shifted segment until every stored segment it needs has arrived or the output has ended (link:0019-recoverable-jit-hls-segment-delivery.adoc[ADR 0019]).

*Why:* In the measurements, a copied AC-3, FLAC or AAC track shifted by moving its decode times kept every packet byte-identical.
Every decode time moved by exactly the delay in ticks, and every decoded click moved by exactly the delay in samples.
Each rebuilt segment's samples all started inside the segment's interval.
A rebuilt segment keeps every sample inside its playlist interval, so players place the shifted audio where the playlist says it is.
The server runs no FFmpeg (link:0033-transcode-worker-is-a-separate-service.adoc[ADR 0033]) and needs none: it rewrites the boxes of the worker's own segment layout.
Evidence: <<evidence-shift>>.

[[decision-change]]
=== A change of delay reloads the player at its position and starts no job attempt

Because the server stores the audio without the delay, a change of delay needs no new audio from the worker.
The running job attempts continue, and the stored video segments serve the reloaded player.

The multivariant playlist's URL, each audio rendition's media playlist URL and each audio segment's URL carry the delay.
The variants' media playlist URLs and video segment URLs do not, so a reload asks for the same video URLs.
The server serves the delay that a URL names, after it checks the range and the step.

To apply a change, the client loads the new `streamUrl` at the current position.
The Apple client replaces the `AVPlayerItem` and seeks the new item to the position.
A seek alone is not enough, because AVPlayer plays out the segments that it has already buffered.
The web client loads the new URL in hls.js with the position as its start position.
The position after the change stays within one video frame of the position before it.
The picture holds for no more than 0.5 s, the bound that both client tickets set.

*Why:* AetherEngine applies a change the same way and measured about 0.3 s of held picture.
A delay applied in the worker would need a new job attempt for every running variant at each change.
The picture would then hold until a new attempt delivered its first segment, and an encoded variant would encode the same video again.

[[decision-audio-lead]]
=== Job attempts deliver an audio lead while the delay is positive

The audio lead is every audio sample of the 2 s of media time before a job attempt's first media segment, for each audio rendition.
The producer delivers the audio lead apart from the attempt's media segments.
Without this decision, the producer discards that audio as preroll (ADR 0037).
The audio lead's length equals the largest delay, so a lead covers any positive delay.

The server asks for the audio lead in a job when the playback session's delay is positive and the server holds no audio segment before the attempt's first segment.
For a stream copy, the attempt's input seek then starts 2 s earlier, and the video before the first segment stays preroll.
For an encode, the audio encode starts on the session's audio frame grid at or before the lead's start, and decodes one period earlier, as every audio encode does (<<decision-prerequisites>>).
The lead's last sample ends where the first segment's first sample starts.

An attempt that starts at segment 0 has no audio before media time zero to deliver.
Its audio lead is 2 s of encoded silence in the rendition's delivered codec, with the rendition's sample rate and channel layout.
The silence ends where the track's first sample starts.
So a positive delay never leaves the start of the title without audio.

When the viewer sets a positive delay and the segment at the player's position needs an audio lead that no attempt delivered, the server starts a job attempt for the current variant at that segment.
That attempt delivers the lead, and the request for the shifted segment waits for it.
An attempt in a session whose delay is 0 or negative delivers no lead, so its seeks read no more of the source.
Under ADR 0033, the server asks for an audio lead only from a worker that advertises support for it.

*Why:* A positive delay moves the sound after a seek later by up to 2 s.
Without the lead, a shifted segment after a seek would start with up to 2 s of picture and no sound.
At media time zero, the source holds no audio before the start, so only encoded silence can fill that time.

[[decision-prerequisites]]
=== The delay requires contiguous audio that the producer never moves

The delay depends on four rules:

. Every audio track is its own audio rendition (ADR 0043, https://github.com/streamarr/streamarr-server/issues/503[streamarr/streamarr-server#503]).
The server offers no delay for a stream session with multiplexed variants.
. Every audio encode starts its input on the session's audio frame grid, counted from the track's probed start.
FLAC and ALAC write fixed blocks of the sample rate ÷ 20.
Each attempt decodes from one period early and trims to the first grid point at or after its boundary.
So the join between two attempts' audio is 0 samples (pending amendment to ADR 0043 in streamarr/streamarr-adr#84).
. The producer never moves a copied track, and encodes start on the copy's grid (pending amendment to ADR 0037).
. The producer rebuilds each copied sample's duration from its sample count, and its decode time from the running sum, for any block size (pending amendment to ADR 0037).

A shifted segment spans the join between two stored segments, which can come from different job attempts.
A `trun` gives each sample a duration, not a start time, so a gap or an overlap at that join cannot be kept inside one segment.
Rules 2 and 4 make every join contiguous.
Where a join still overlaps, the server drops the later delivery's overlapping samples.
Where a join leaves a gap, the server lengthens the duration of the sample before the gap, so every later sample keeps its own start.
The server counts each such join in a metric.

Rule 3 makes the delay the only intended move of a copied track.
With encodes on the copy's grid, every audio rendition of the session shares one timing, so one delay suits every rendition.
Two copied tracks whose frames sit on different grids each keep their authored timing.
A switch between them plays each track at its own authored time, and the delay adds the same amount to both.

*Why:* ADR 0037's half-frame snap moved a copied track by up to 16 ms for AC-3 and E-AC-3.
The snap moved a copied AC-3 track with an authored delay of +12 ms a further 14.7 ms later.
A viewer would tune the delay against that move, and the move differs from track to track.
In the measurements, AAC copied from Matroska carried 3 frames of 432 with durations of 1,014 to 1,022 samples, because Matroska stores times in whole milliseconds.
After a cut by a −1,230 ms delay, its clicks decoded 9 samples (0.2 ms) early.
With every duration at 1,024 samples, the same cut moved every click by exactly the delay.
Evidence: <<evidence-shift>>.

[[decision-direct-play]]
=== In Original, a delay other than 0 plays the HLS copy instead of direct play

Direct play sends the source file unchanged, so the server cannot shift its audio.
When the playback session's delay is not 0, Original skips direct play whenever one capability entry accepts the HLS copy (link:0049-capability-declarations-list-entries-and-an-output-fits-one-entry.adoc[ADR 0049]).
Original then starts with the HLS copy, which carries the same video and the same audio packets.
This applies to the first format, to the fallback order of ADR 0052 and to a viewer who picks Original again.
When no capability entry accepts the HLS copy, Original direct-plays the file without the delay.
The client then shows that the delay does not apply to this title in Original.
The server never encodes the video for the sake of the delay.

When the viewer sets a delay other than 0 during direct play, the client starts the HLS copy at the current position.
The change is the viewer's own choice, not format recovery.
It spends no format attempt and keeps the playback session's format-failure history.
A change back to 0 keeps the HLS copy playing.

*Why:* tvOS starts in Original and direct-plays every file that its declaration covers (ADR 0045).
The Apple TV is Streamarr's primary client, so a delay that did not apply in direct play would not apply on the primary client.
The HLS copy changes no video or audio sample.
An encode would trade picture quality for the delay, and a viewer who wants that can pick Auto.

[[decision-recovery]]
=== Format recovery keeps the delay

Each new format attempt of ADR 0052 starts its stream session with the playback session's delay.
A change of delay spends no format attempt and never resets the format-failure history.
A viewer who picks Original again keeps the delay.
A format error in a stream whose audio is shifted takes the same recovery path as any other format error.

*Why:* The delay belongs to the viewer's chain, which a format attempt does not change.

[[decision-deferred]]
=== Explicitly deferred

* A client-side delay during direct play for a file that no HLS copy fits.
* A suggested starting delay from the latency that a platform reports, such as `AVAudioSession.outputLatency`.
* A per-title correction for a file whose own audio is out of sync.
* The setting in the Android TV client.

== Consequences

* A viewer on any client can move the sound up to 2 s earlier or later in 10 ms steps.
Every channel, every codec and every bit-identical copy stays as it is.
* The server gains a writer for the worker's audio segment layout, which reads the stored samples and writes shifted segments.
Its tests compare every packet's bytes before and after a shift.
* The worker gains the audio lead and the silent lead at media time zero, behind an advertised capability.
* With a positive delay, a copy attempt's seek reads 2 s more of the source.
* With a negative delay, a start or a seek waits for the next segment's audio as well.
At 3 s segments and the speed floor of about 2.10× real time, an encode delivers that segment up to about 1.4 s later.
A copy runs faster than the speed floor.
* With a negative delay, the first part of the title's audio, up to 2 s, is never heard, and the audio ends up to 2 s before the picture.
* With a delay other than 0, Original on tvOS plays the HLS copy, which costs one worker remux job per stream session.
* Each client's merge check runs a synthetic stream with a click every second and a flash on the same frame.
At +200 ms and −200 ms, the decoded click moves by the set amount within ±10 ms against the flash.
The check also covers a start at media time zero at +2,000 ms, a seek at +2,000 ms, the end of the title at −2,000 ms, and a rendition switch and a quality switch at a set delay.
The web check runs in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit headless, and the Apple check runs on an iPhone.
The Apple TV and SHIELD checks run once at the end of the epic and gate nothing.
They include an E-AC-3 copy with Atmos through a receiver at +200 ms, to hear whether the silent lead switches the receiver out of Atmos at the start.

== Rejected Alternatives

* A client-side delay with an `MTAudioProcessingTap` or a Web Audio `DelayNode` — it can only delay, so a negative delay is impossible, and a tap may make AVPlayer decode E-AC-3 that would otherwise pass through.
* An AVPlayer audio offset — AVPlayer has none, and `outputLatency` is read-only.
* Two synchronized AVPlayers, one for the video and one for the audio rendition — the video player still downloads its own audio, and the drift between two players is unmeasured.
* An own renderer on `AVSampleBufferRenderSynchronizer` — it leaves the platform player, against ADR 0045.
* The worker applies the delay in the job — each change needs new job attempts, and the picture holds until they deliver.
* A new stream session for each change — the same cost as a delay in the worker, with a new segment store as well.
* Moving the decode times without moving samples between segments — each segment's audio would lie up to 2 s outside its playlist interval, so a player that seeks by the playlist would fetch the wrong audio segment.
* An edit list in the initialization segment for each delay — an audio rendition's initialization segment must be the same for every job attempt (ADR 0037), and edit lists are not yet verified in hls.js and on the Apple TV.
* Shifting the video instead of the audio — each segment opens at a video keyframe inside its interval (ADR 0037), and the subtitles would have to move with the picture.
* Resampling or re-encoding a copy to apply the delay — it would destroy the bit-identical copy.
* Storing the delay with the Account or the Profile — it describes one device's chain.
* Storing the delay with the device registration on the server — the server does not see which audio output the device uses.
* A range beyond ±2 s — it would need a longer audio lead, and Apple names about 2 s as the AirPlay worst case.

== Evidence

[[evidence-shift]]
=== Moving decode times keeps every packet and moves the sound exactly

*Setup:* FFmpeg 8.1.3 at `/layers/com.streamarr.ffmpeg/ffmpeg/bin/ffmpeg` in `streamarr/streamarr-transcode-worker@sha256:f2011e95de3323e4b31de2fd3e22c0bee980ee2d2993ac404aa1bc5ac7d78691`.
The sources were synthetic 10 s click trains with a 1 ms click every second:

* AC-3 at 448 kb/s, stereo, 48 kHz, in Matroska;
* FLAC, mono, 48 kHz, in Matroska;
* AAC at 192 kb/s, mono, 44.1 kHz, in Matroska, and the same AAC written straight to fragmented MP4.

FFmpeg copied each track into fragmented MP4 with 3 s fragments.
A script then did what the server does: it added the delay to every sample's start and rebuilt one `moof` and one `mdat` per 3 s interval.
The delays were +730 ms and −1,230 ms.
`-f framemd5` with `-copyts` compared the packets, and a decode with timestamps honoured located each click.

*Results:*

* Every packet kept its bytes and its size, in all 1,503 packets compared across the five shifted files.
* Every decode time moved by exactly the delay: 35,040 and −59,040 ticks at 48 kHz, and 32,193 and −54,243 ticks at 44.1 kHz.
* In all 18 rebuilt segments, every sample started inside the segment's interval.
* Every decoded AC-3 and FLAC click moved by exactly the delay in samples.
For AAC written straight to MP4, every click moved by exactly the delay at both delays.
* The −1,230 ms delay dropped the audio before media time zero: 39 AC-3 frames and 53 AAC frames.
* AAC copied from Matroska moved by exactly the delay at +730 ms.
At −1,230 ms, its clicks decoded 9 samples early, because 3 of its 432 frames carried durations of 1,014 to 1,022 samples.

= ADR 0064: Copied audio ends playback at its last media segment

== Status

Accepted

== Context

An HLS copy can advertise video after its selected audio has ended.
A wholly silent final segment can leave a browser waiting for audio, and a seek into that tail can begin an attempt with no audio packet from which to initialize its copy.
link:0037-worker-delivers-fmp4-segments-from-ffmpeg-stdout.adoc[ADR 0037] previously encoded audio that ended at least one segment early and accepted this limitation for a shorter tail.
That threshold changes the audio for the whole title without ensuring that every advertised segment contains audio.
Keeping the copied bytes and ending at a media segment boundary trades the remaining silent picture for a common end that the playlist and the worker can enforce.

== Decision

This decision applies to an HLS stream session whose variants multiplex one selected audio track with video.
When that track is eligible for copying and its header or a scan supplies its end, the server keeps the copy and ends playback at the end of the media segment containing the last audio sample.
An audio end exactly on a segment boundary belongs to the preceding segment.
The boundary comes from the initially chosen video format's segment timeline, including merged final segments, and never extends the title beyond its otherwise selected media end.
The rule applies whether the video is copied or encoded.
The selected audio's known early end alone no longer requires an audio encode.
Other reasons to encode the audio still apply; encoded audio retains ADR 0037's padding behavior.

The chosen end stays fixed across later format attempts of that playback, including a recovery that changes the video format or segment spacing.
Every variant advertises that end and sends it to every job attempt.
The playlist exposes that end, and watch progress measures completion against it.
A resume at or beyond the chosen end is already finished and starts no job attempt.
The API supplies the terminal end for that finished resume, and the client reports it without waiting for media; playing again begins a fresh playback from the beginning.
An ordinary planned playlist end is not a terminal short-copy report, so temporary buffering before it never counts as completion.
Selecting a different audio track in a new stream session chooses an end for that track.
Direct play and HLS audio-rendition layouts retain their existing policies.

The worker keeps video only through the advertised end.
For copied video with reordered pictures, it retains a valid decode-order prefix; a later leading picture whose presentation time precedes the boundary cannot reintroduce a reference to a discarded picture.
The copied audio's packet payloads stay bit-identical, with no padding, resampling or re-encoding.
Once the final advertised segment is closed and every delivered segment is acknowledged, an active attempt may complete by intentionally stopping FFmpeg at this boundary.
That planned completion does not require a natural end of file or exit status zero from the intentionally stopped process.
It reports no learned source-stream end, because the source may continue.
A refusal or cancellation before acceptance of the final delivery retains its failed or stopped outcome.
Natural end-of-file completion still follows ADR 0037's clean-process-exit rules.

An end learned only from a job attempt is not a stated or scanned end for this decision.
FFmpeg can stop at a read error and still exit successfully, so that evidence must not silently shorten future playback.
Unknown ends retain ADR 0037's failed-copy recovery to a padded encode, including an attempt that cannot initialize a copied track after that track has ended.
The worker adds no refusal solely for a known audio end, a late audio start, or a gap inside copied audio.
Damaged media remains subject to the existing bounded failure and recovery rules.

== Consequences

The final segment can contain picture after its last audio sample, but no later wholly silent segment is advertised.
The picture after that boundary is intentionally unavailable in this playback.
The selected end is playback state, rather than a replacement for the file's probe duration or a learned source end.
Format recovery, immediate worker failure, resume and replay must all preserve that distinction.

Clean-room fixtures with generated pictures and tones verify copied AAC and FLAC packet identity and normal completion of a partly-audio final segment.
Normal playback, seeking into its silent portion and starting there passed in Chromium, Firefox and actual Safari.
Playwright's Linux WebKit passed normal playback but did not complete the tested seek and start cases; it is not evidence that those cases pass in that engine.
Copied open-GOP video also requires decoded-picture comparison, because a decoder can conceal a missing reference without reporting a hard decode failure.

== Rejected Alternatives

* Encode whenever audio ends before the last segment — changes the audio for the whole title to preserve a silent picture tail.
* Advertise the silent tail and end only after a client stall — leaves a player-dependent recovery path and can start an audio copy with no packet.
* Cut exactly at the audio end — moves the end inside a segment whose video may require a later reference picture.
* Treat an intentional stop as a learned source end — mistakes a delivery boundary for evidence about the file.

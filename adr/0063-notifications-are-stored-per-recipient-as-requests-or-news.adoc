= ADR 0063: A Notification Keeps What Its Recipient Was Told, as a Request or as News, and the Domain That Publishes an Event Decides Who Is Told and What Is Dismissed

== Status

Proposed

This decision amends link:0042-graphql-subscriptions-run-over-websocket-until-token-expiry.adoc[ADR 0042]: `notificationsChanged` needs an Account session that is not device-bound, with or without a selected Profile.
https://github.com/streamarr/streamarr-server/issues/873[streamarr/streamarr-server#873] implements the first build.

== Context

Some things in Streamarr wait for a person.
A share offer waits for a HouseholdAdmin of its target Household, and a ProfileManager invitation waits for the Account that it invites (link:0024-identity-authority-by-relationship.adoc[ADR 0024]).
Other things only need telling: an offer was accepted, a share ended, a scan finished.
Today a person learns about either only by opening the page that lists it.

Three decisions left this open.
ADR 0024 deferred notifications together with its screens and GraphQL contracts.
link:0013-stream-decision-architecture.adoc[ADR 0013] deferred a notification when a viewer shares a playback report.
link:0010-spring-application-events.adoc[ADR 0010] expected its application events to feed pushes to the interface.

GraphQL subscriptions (ADR 0042) do not fill the gap.
A subscription keeps an open screen current.
It stores nothing, so a person who is not looking misses the change.
https://github.com/streamarr/streamarr-server/issues/871[streamarr/streamarr-server#871] adds live updates of shares to the sharing pages, and those updates too reach only open screens.

The people around a subject change over time.
A HouseholdAdmin can be demoted or move to another Household, a ProfileManager can be removed or relinquish management, and a ServerAdmin can lose that authority (ADR 0024).
What a person was told stays what they were told, but a person who can no longer decide something must not be left with a request to decide it.

Each subject already has an owner that decides who may read it.
For a share, that is the sharing rule of ADR 0024, which Cedar decides (link:0025-cedar-decides-authorization.adoc[ADR 0025]).
A record of what someone was told should not decide that a second time.

Some events repeat.
A scheduled scan can run every day (link:0027-library-operations-are-durable-jobs.adoc[ADR 0027]), and a library can keep unmatched items from one scan to the next.

The web client will list Notifications in the Profile menu, with an unread count and a dot on the avatar.
That design is recorded elsewhere.
Email and the TV apps come later and must fit without a new model.
Streamarr has no production instances.

== Decision

A Notification is a stored record of what one recipient was told when something happened to a subject.
Each recipient gets its own copy when the event happens, with its own read state.
A Notification is either a request, which waits for a decision on its subject, or news, which needs no decision.
The domain that publishes an event decides once who is told and what they are told.
The notification module stores, coalesces, lists, counts, marks, pushes, dismisses and retains, and it decides nothing about a subject.

=== What a Notification is, and is not

A Notification has one recipient, one kind and one subject.
The kind says what happened.
It fixes the Notification's form, request or news, and the facts that its snapshot holds.
The subject is the record that the Notification is about, such as one share offer or one library.
The Notification keeps a reference to its subject, and that reference is its link target.
The snapshot holds what the recipient was told, such as the Profile's name and the answer to an offer.

* A Notification is a record of what its recipient was told, not a view of its subject.
  The server never works its content out again from the subject, and no later change to its recipient's relationships changes it.
* A Notification is not the source of truth for anything that waits.
  The pending offers into a Household, the pending invitations of an Account and the unmatched items of a library are counted from the offers, the invitations and the items.
  A Notification can point to such a list, and it never changes what the list shows.
* A Notification is not a subscription event.
  A subscription event updates an open screen and is stored nowhere; a Notification stays stored until retention or a dismissal deletes it.
* No person writes a Notification.
  Its text comes from its kind and its snapshot.
* A Notification is not an audit record.
  Retention deletes Notifications, and the security audit (ADR 0024) keeps the record of each action.

Every recipient today is an Account.
A Profile may become a recipient later, for example for the TV apps, and a decision of its own will say how.
This decision stores the recipient as a reference, as it stores the subject, so nothing here stands in that decision's way.

In this ADR, "request" names a Notification's form.
An API request keeps its usual meaning.

=== Who is told is decided once, when the event happens

The domain that publishes an event decides who is told and what they are told.
It works out the kind's recipients from the relationships as they stand at that moment, inside the transaction that changes the subject.
It holds the relationship rows that it reads, as ADR 0024 requires of every operation that decides from relationships.
A relationship that changes at the same moment therefore either waits until the event commits or has already changed when the event reads it.
The Notification commits or rolls back with the change that made it.

A recipient is told only what it may know at that moment: what it may read of the subject then, or, when the event ends its access, what it could read until that event.
Each kind's recipients below follow this rule.

* The Account that acts never receives a Notification about its own action.
  A HouseholdAdmin who accepts an offer gets no news of the acceptance, even when that HouseholdAdmin also manages the Profile.
* A disabled Account still receives its copies.
  Disabling an Account changes no role or grant (ADR 0024), and the Account reads its Notifications after it is enabled again.
* An Account that stands in two relationships to one subject receives one copy.

Nothing decides again later.
Listing, counting, marking and pushing a Notification look at the Notification and its recipient alone, and never at anyone's relationships.
A person who later loses the relationship through which it was told keeps the news it was told.
A waiting request is the one thing that a lost relationship takes away, through a dismissal by the domain that owns the relationship (see <<dismissal>>).

*Why a copy for each recipient:* each recipient has a read state of its own, so each needs a record of its own.
A copy also fixes who was told: an Account that joins a group later does not inherit what was said to the group before.

*Why decide once:* the domain that owns a subject already decides who may read it, at the moment that it publishes the event.
Deciding again at each read would state that rule a second time in the notification module and read the subject through a second path, only to take back what the reader was already told.

==== The accepted gap

A person who gains a role later gets no Notification for what was already waiting.
A new HouseholdAdmin receives no request for an offer that was made before the promotion.
The new HouseholdAdmin still sees the offer on the Household's page, because that page counts the offers themselves.
A HouseholdAdmin who is demoted and then promoted again likewise gets no dismissed request back.

=== Requests and news

Each kind has one form, and the form never changes.

A request waits for a decision on its subject.
Its state is stored on the request, and only events write it:

* The request waits from when it is made until an event resolves it or its subject's expiry passes.
* The request resolves when its subject stops waiting, for every recipient at once:
** _decided_, when anyone with the authority accepts, rejects or declines the subject, and the request stores which;
** _withdrawn_, when its proposer cancels the subject, a newer proposal replaces it, or the server invalidates it (ADR 0024), and the request does not store which;
** _expired_, when the subject's expiry passes with no decision.
* The domain that owns the subject publishes each resolution in the transaction that changes the subject.
  The resolution carries the instant of that change: the one instant that the subject's domain takes for the change, checks the subject's expiry against and records on the subject.
  The module writes the state and that instant to every request about that subject that no event has resolved yet.
* A request stores its subject's expiry, which the event that makes it carries, so it reads as expired from its own data once that time passes, before any event says so.
  A subject's expiry never changes: a change of the lifetime setting applies only to offers and invitations made after it (ADR 0024).
* The module compares the resolution's instant with the stored expiry, never the time at which it handles the resolution.
  A resolution whose instant is before the expiry is written as it is, even when it reaches the module after the expiry, for example because its transaction waited for locks.
  An offer or an invitation waits only for its lifetime (ADR 0024), so its domain accepts, rejects or declines it only at an instant before its expiry, and an acceptance never leaves an active share beside an expired request.
  A request that read as expired while such a resolution was on its way then reads as decided or withdrawn, and the change is pushed like any other resolution.
  A resolution whose instant is at or after the expiry is written as expired, at the expiry, so a request that already read as expired goes on reading so.
* A resolved request counts as read.
  It stays in the list as history, with how it resolved, and it never becomes unread again.
* A recipient may mark a waiting request read.
  Reading decides nothing: the request shows that it waits until it resolves.
* A request's subject waits only once.
  A newer proposal is a new subject, with requests of its own.

News needs no decision.
It stays unread until its recipient marks it read, and nothing else marks it read.

*Why a request stores its state:* the module reads no other module's tables, so it cannot read the subject.
The subject's domain already publishes an event for every change of the subject (see <<live-changes>>), and that event writes the state.
An event that a bug left out would leave a request waiting for something already settled, but only until its stored expiry, from which it reads as expired.

=== One unread news per kind, subject and recipient

A recipient has at most one unread news of each kind about one subject.
A later event of that kind about that subject replaces the unread news' snapshot and its time.
Apart from a request's resolution, that is the only way that a Notification's content changes.
The Notification keeps its identity, stays unread and moves to the top of the list.
The new snapshot is the later event's own, except for the one kind whose row in the table below says that it adds the later event's figures to the stored ones.
When the recipient has no unread news of that kind about that subject, the event makes a new Notification.
A read Notification is history, and no later event changes it.

For example, a scan of a library named Movies leaves 14 unmatched items, and each ServerAdmin gets "Movies: 14 titles need matching".
A later scan that adds more unmatched items replaces the snapshot of the same unread Notification, and its line shows the number that the library held after that scan, such as 20.
The ServerAdmin still has one Notification about it.
After a ServerAdmin reads it, the next such scan makes a new Notification for that ServerAdmin.

Different kinds of news about one subject sit side by side.
An Account offers a Profile to a Household.
Before the Account next looks, the Household accepts the offer, and a week later it ends the share.
The Account then has two unread Notifications about the share, "Offer answered" and "Share ended", and the second does not replace the first.
Only an event of a Notification's own kind updates it, so a Notification never changes its kind or its GraphQL type.

Requests never update in place.
A request's subject waits only once, and a newer proposal is a new subject, so no later event of a request's kind is about the same subject.

PostgreSQL holds the rule with a unique index over the unread news of each recipient, kind and subject, so two events at the same moment update one Notification.
A second unique index allows one request of each kind about one subject for each recipient, so an event that is published twice makes no second request.

[[order-and-paging]]
=== The list's order and paging

The list holds the reader's Notifications, newest first.
A Notification's time is the time of its latest event, `latestEventAt`, so a Notification that is updated in place moves to the top.
A request keeps its time when it resolves, because a resolution is not an event of the request's kind.
Notifications with the same time are ordered by their id, in the same direction.

The list uses the server's shared pagination (link:0014-protocol-agnostic-pagination.adoc[ADR 0014]) and all of its rules, and it adds no paging of its own.
It is a `PagedList` under the shared `Pager`, ordered by `latestEventAt` descending, and it reads its rows in SQL through the shared jOOQ `Keyset` query.
Its cursors are the shared position cursors of `PositionCursorCodec`: each holds a position, which is a time and an id, with the list's name and the Account as its scope, so it continues only that Account's list.
A cursor that cannot continue the list fails with `INVALID_CURSOR`, as on every list.
Its edges implement `OrderedEdge`, because the list publishes live changes, and its integration test extends the shared `PagedListContract`.

A cursor holds a position, never a Notification.
When the Notification that a cursor names has since moved to the top or been dismissed, no row is left at the cursor's position.
The next page then starts where that Notification was, as ADR 0014 says for an item that has left a list.
A moved Notification now sits above the cursor, so a later page does not show it again.
A client places it at the top from its live change, by its edge's `orderKey`, and a list read again from the start shows it there.

=== Read and unread

Read state belongs to each recipient's own copy, and marking a Notification read changes no other recipient's copy.
A playback report works differently: it is one shared record, so it is read for every ServerAdmin once any ServerAdmin marks it read (ADR 0013).

Read state changes in two ways only:

* `markNotificationsRead` marks Notifications read by their ids.
  The client sends the ids of the rows that its page showed, from 1 to 100 of them.
  The server marks only the caller's own unread Notifications among them.
  It skips an id that does not exist, a dismissed one included, that is another Account's or that is already read, with no error, and its answer does not say which ids it skipped.
  Sending the same ids again therefore changes nothing and reports nothing, as link:0026-mutation-payloads-and-error-channels.adoc[ADR 0026] requires of a naturally idempotent mutation.
  A Notification that the page did not show is never marked by that call, so a Notification that commits after the page was read stays unread, whatever its time.
* A request becomes read when it resolves, or when its stored expiry passes.

Opening the list marks nothing.
A client marks a Notification read when the reader opens it, by sending that one id.
When the reader marks them all, the client sends the ids of every row that it shows, in more than one call when it shows more than 100.

The unread count counts the caller's unread news and the caller's requests that still wait and that the caller has not marked read.

[[what-it-stores]]
=== What a Notification stores

A Notification stores its recipient, its kind, its form and the reference to its subject.
It stores when it was made, when its latest event happened, and when its recipient marked it read.
A request also stores its state, when it resolved, and when its subject expires.
The snapshot holds the rest: the facts of the event that the line needs, such as the Profile's name, an offer's answer, which side ended a share, or a scan's figures.
The kind's row in the table below lists them.

The snapshot is what the recipient was told, and nothing reads the subject to change it.
Every name in a line comes from the snapshot.
The sharing kinds and the Profile management kinds keep one name, the Profile's, as it was at the Notification's latest event, and the library kinds keep the library's name.
A Profile's name is kept alone, with no picture and no kind.
A Profile renamed later keeps its old name in older Notifications, for every recipient.

A recipient keeps the name that it was told, whatever becomes of its relationships, until retention or a dismissal deletes the Notification.
For the Household's side of a share, this is the one place where the Profile's name outlives the offer or the share.
ADR 0024, as https://github.com/streamarr/streamarr-adr/pull/127[streamarr/streamarr-adr#127] amends it, lets that side see the Profile's name, picture and kind, and the offerer's name, only while the offer is pending or the share is active, and it records the kept name as its one exception.
A Notification keeps no other name: not the offerer's, not the Household's, and not the inviter's.
Each of those would outlive what ADR 0024 lets its recipient see of it.
A client that wants them opens the subject by its reference, through the ordinary queries, which answer by the subject's own read rule and may answer that it does not exist.

From its snapshot, its times and its state, a Notification gives one line of text and a link target, and later an email.
The server also writes that line in English, for a client that shows a type but handles it no further.
Each client may word its own line from the typed fields.
The server sends no URL: each client maps the subject's reference to its own screen, and a later email builds its link from the same reference.

A Notification never carries a secret, such as an invitation code, a share code or a pairing code.

[[dismissal]]
=== Dismissal

A dismissal deletes Notifications before retention would.
It is one general operation of the module, and a domain asks for it with an event, as it asks for a Notification to be made.
A dismissed Notification leaves its recipient's list and unread count for good.
It is deleted, not kept in a third state beside read and unread, so no read has to leave it out and nothing brings it back.

A dismissal carries a selector, which selects either of these:

* Notifications by id, one or more.
* Every Notification about one or more subjects, each named by its subject reference.

Either may be narrowed to one recipient, to one or more kinds, and to one form: news, or the requests that no event has resolved yet.
A selector always names ids or subjects, so no dismissal selects every Notification.

The module deletes what the selector selects and asks nothing about why.
The domain that dismisses owns the reason: it works out the selector from its own records, and it selects no more than it means to.

A dismissal runs inside the transaction of the event that causes it, so it commits or rolls back with that event.
After commit, each recipient that lost a Notification receives one live change with each dismissed id and its new unread count (see <<live-changes>>), so its open lists and its count update without a read.

Its first uses:

* *A request that is no longer the recipient's to decide.*
  When an Account loses the relationship through which a waiting request reached it, the domain that owns that relationship dismisses that Account's waiting requests about the subjects concerned.
  Today that relationship is a HouseholdAdmin's role in an offer's target Household.
  When a HouseholdAdmin loses that role, or its Account moves to another Household, the identity domain changes the role and then, in the same transaction, dismisses that Account's requests about the offers still pending into that Household.
  It names those offers by their references and narrows the selector to that Account and to the requests that no event has resolved.
  A request kind that later reaches an Account as a ProfileManager is dismissed the same way when that Account stops managing the Profile.
  The module stores no audience and no side for this, because the identity domain knows which offers wait for that Household.
  News that the Account was told stays, and so do its resolved requests.
  An offer made at the same moment as a demotion holds the role rows that it reads, so the two run one after the other.
  When the demotion runs first, the offer reads the changed role and does not tell that Account; when the offer commits first, the demotion's dismissal sees the new request and deletes it.
  A change that ends the subject itself resolves its requests instead: an invitation that the server invalidates because its invited Account became ineligible leaves that Account a withdrawn request.
* *A deleted subject.*
  Deleting a subject dismisses every Notification about it, for every recipient, in the transaction that deletes it.
  Deleting a share dismisses the Notifications about it, and deleting a Household dismisses those about it and about the shares into it.
  Deleting a Profile dismisses the Notifications about the Profile, its shares and its ProfileManager invitations, because nothing of a deleted Profile stays anywhere, its name in an old Notification included (ADR 0024).
  An invitation keeps its row when its Profile is deleted (ADR 0024), and its Notifications are dismissed all the same.
  Removing a library dismisses the Notifications about the library.

Later uses fit the same selector, and this decision builds none of them:

* A person dismisses one of their own Notifications from a client: a mutation dismisses by id, narrowed to the caller as recipient.
* A later event makes news pointless: once a library holds no unmatched items, the library domain could dismiss its _Unmatched items_ news, by the library's reference, narrowed to that kind.
* News about one shared record, such as news to every ServerAdmin when a viewer shares a playback report (ADR 0013), could be dismissed for every ServerAdmin once one of them marks the report read, by the report's reference, narrowed to that kind.

=== Deletion, disabling, and the end of a subject

* *A subject is deleted:* its Notifications are dismissed, for every recipient (see <<dismissal>>).
  When a share ends because its Profile or its Household is deleted, the share is deleted too, and nobody gets news.
* *A recipient Account is deleted:* its Notifications are deleted with it, in the transaction that deletes the Account.
  Nothing is pushed, because the Account's sessions end with it.
  No other Account's Notification holds the deleted Account's name, so the deletion of the Account alone changes no other Notification.
  A Personal Profile that is erased with its Account is a deleted Profile, and the Notifications about it are dismissed.
* *An Account is disabled:* nothing changes for its Notifications.
  The Account keeps receiving copies.
  Its open sockets close when their access tokens expire (ADR 0042), and it cannot sign in to read them until it is enabled again (ADR 0024).
* *An Account loses a relationship:* its news stays, and the domain that owns the relationship dismisses the requests that are no longer the Account's to decide (see <<dismissal>>).
* *An offer is canceled, replaced or invalidated:* its requests resolve as withdrawn.
  The offering side gets no news, because its own side or the server withdrew the offer.
* *An offer expires:* its requests read as expired from their stored expiry, and nobody gets news.
  The identity domain resolves them as expired when the expiry sweep stores the offer's expiry, and that resolution is pushed (see <<live-changes>>).
* *A share ends:* a side that did not end it gets news, as the sharing kinds below set out.

[[retention]]
=== Retention

The server keeps 90 days of history by default.
History is the news that its recipient read and the requests that resolved, a request that reads as expired from its stored expiry included.
The server deletes such a Notification once its latest event is older than the retention period.

Unread news and waiting requests stay.
News stays until it is read, and unread news stays few, because the noise rule allows one unread news for each kind and subject.
Every request built now waits for a subject that expires, so every waiting request becomes history, even one whose resolution was never published.

A recurring scheduled task deletes old history once a day, under the system actor, a batch at a time.
Each delete states the conditions of history and age in its `WHERE`.
History never stops being history: read news is never updated in place, and a request that reads as resolved never reads as waiting again.
The task therefore takes no lock and reads nothing beyond its own rows, and two servers that run it together delete each row once.

The retention period is a server-wide setting.
A ServerAdmin reads and changes it within the bounds that the setting reports, as with the idle segment retention setting.

=== Who may use Notifications

The server serves Notifications to every Account session that is not device-bound.
The session may be Account-scoped or Profile-scoped, with or without a selected Profile, and may use any Household that the Account may use (ADR 0024).
Each session reads only the Notifications of its own Account, and nothing else decides what it reads.
The web client is the only client that shows them now.

A web session is the Account's own sign-in.
Whichever Profile it selects, a Kid Profile on a shared computer included, it acts with the Account's authority, which never comes from the selected Profile (ADR 0024).
Its Notifications show what was told to that Account and nothing else.

Device-bound sessions receive none.
A Cedar guardrail forbids every Notification action to a session that carries a registration id, as ADR 0025 forbids such sessions every administration action.
A shared TV runs on the session of the Account that linked it while other people use it, so Notifications on it would show everyone in the room what was told to one Account.
This rule is about the session, not about a relationship.

No query reads another Account's Notifications, and that includes a ServerAdmin's queries.
A ServerAdmin sees the subjects themselves.

[[sharing-kinds]]
==== The sharing kinds

An offer becomes the share when it is accepted: one `ProfileShare` goes from pending to active to ended (ADR 0024).
That one record is the subject of all three sharing kinds, and each of them stores the share's id as its subject's reference.

A share has two sides, and the identity domain works out each from the relationships as they stand at the event:

* The offering side: the Profile's ProfileManagers, direct and derived, and the Account that made the offer, which the share records, while that Account may still read the share.
  The Account of a self-managed Personal Profile is one of the managers.
  The Account that made the offer need not manage the Profile: a ServerAdmin may offer any Profile, and an Account invitation's automatic re-offer of a restricted Profile is made in the name of the invitation's issuer, a ServerAdmin.
  An Account that made the offer and has since stopped managing the Profile, and is not a ServerAdmin, may no longer read the share (ADR 0024), so it is not on the offering side.
* The Household's side: the target Household's HouseholdAdmins.

An Account can stand on both sides, and it still receives one copy.
The snapshot is the same for both sides, so the copy needs to record no side.

_Offer waiting_ (`ProfileShareOfferedNotification`, a request).
Every offer makes one for each HouseholdAdmin of the target Household, except the Account that made the offer.
They may see the Profile's name while the offer is pending (ADR 0024).
An offer that replaces an older one withdraws the older offer's requests and makes new ones.
A ServerAdmin may decide any offer, but a ServerAdmin receives no request for it.

The first build follows https://github.com/streamarr/streamarr-server/issues/870[streamarr/streamarr-server#870].
With it, every ordinary offer spends a share code that the target Household issued, and an Account invitation's automatic re-offers go only to Households that already host the Profile (streamarr/streamarr-adr#127).
Each request in a Household's list then answers a code that the Household handed out.
Without share codes, any ProfileManager could fill another Household's lists by offering and canceling again and again, because each replacement is a new offer with new requests.

_Offer answered_ (`ProfileShareAnsweredNotification`, news).
When a HouseholdAdmin or a ServerAdmin accepts or rejects an offer, each Account on the offering side gets one, except the Account that answered.
The Account that made the offer is always on the offering side at that moment, because an offer whose offerer loses management or ServerAdmin authority is invalidated while it is pending (ADR 0024).

_Share ended_ (`ProfileShareEndedNotification`, news).
Who ended the share decides who is told:

* When a HouseholdAdmin of the Household ends the share, the Household's side ended it, and the offering side is told.
  An Account that both administers the Household and manages the Profile ends the share for the Household's side.
* When any other ProfileManager of the Profile ends the share, the Profile's own Account included, the offering side ended it, and the Household's side is told.
* When a ServerAdmin ends the share with `administrativelyEndProfileShare`, or another operation ends it on its way, both sides are told.
  Such operations include an Account transfer that ends the share into the previous Household, and connecting a Profile to an Account, which ends the Profile's other shares (ADR 0024).
* The Account that acted is never told.

The Household's side is told at the moment that it receives the change that ends the share, its last change of that share (ADR 0024, ADR 0042).
It keeps its _Share ended_ news and its resolved _Offer waiting_ requests after the offer or the share is over, and after it stops administering the Household, because they are what it was told.
The share queries still answer it as if the share did not exist (ADR 0024).
A Notification shows it nothing of the share but its own snapshot: the Profile's name, the answer or which side ended the share, and the times.

==== The Profile management kinds

These kinds arrive with the screens for ProfileManager invitations.
Their subject is one ProfileManager invitation, or the Profile for _Management removed_, and they reach the live schema when Profile management is built.

_Invitation received_ (`ProfileManagerInvitationReceivedNotification`, a request).
Issuing an invitation makes one for the Account that it invites, when that Account may accept it.
An invitation that nobody can accept, because no Account has its address, or because the Account that has it is not eligible or already manages the Profile, makes no request (ADR 0024).
A newer invitation to the same address for the same Profile replaces the older one: replacement goes by the address that the inviter typed, never by the Account (ADR 0024).
The older request then resolves as withdrawn, and the newer invitation makes a new request.
Two invitations sent to two addresses can therefore wait for one Account, for example after the Account changed its email.
Each has its own request, and neither withdraws the other.
When the Account accepts one of them, or becomes or stops being one of the Profile's ProfileManagers by any other route, the server invalidates the other (ADR 0024), and its request resolves as withdrawn.
The Notification never carries the invitation's code.
The request opens the page where the invited Account accepts or declines, and that page needs no code, because the invitation already names the Account that may decide (ADR 0024).
The link that carries the code is a second route to the same page, for an inviter who wants to send one.

_Invitation answered_ (`ProfileManagerInvitationAnsweredNotification`, news).
When the invited Account accepts or declines, the Account that issued the invitation gets one.
That Account still manages the Profile at that moment, because an invitation whose inviter loses management is invalidated while it is pending (ADR 0024).

_Management removed_ (`ProfileManagementRemovedNotification`, news).
When the Profile's own Account or a ServerAdmin removes a direct manager, the removed Account gets one.
An Account that relinquishes its own management gets none, because it acted itself.
The removed Account may no longer see the Profile, and it is told the name that it saw until the removal.
A removed Account can accept a new ProfileManager invitation and be granted management again, so it can lose management of one Profile more than once.
The noise rule holds for this kind as for any news: an Account has at most one unread Notification about the removal of its management of one Profile.
A removal while that Notification is unread replaces its time and the Profile's name with the new removal's, and it stays unread.
Once the Account has read it, whether or not retention has deleted it since, a later removal makes a new one.

An invitation keeps its row when its Profile, its inviter or its invited Account is deleted (ADR 0024).
Deleting the Profile dismisses the invitation's Notifications.
Deleting the inviter or the invited Account deletes that Account's own Notifications as a recipient, and no other Notification holds its name.

[[library-kinds]]
==== The library kinds, which are specified only

These two kinds show that the model is not shaped around sharing alone.
They are not in the live schema, and a later decision builds them.
Only a ServerAdmin can scan, refresh or fix a match, so they go to every Account that is a ServerAdmin when the operation finishes.

Both kinds take the library as their subject.
The noise rule counts each kind on its own, so a ServerAdmin can have one unread Notification of each kind about one library, and neither replaces the other.
The unmatched kind opens its library under the `unmatched` filter.

`ScanEndedEvent` and `RefreshEndedEvent` carry only the library's id, and the server publishes them for every conclusion.
The decision that builds these kinds adds the library operation's id to them, and the library domain reads the conclusion and the figures from that library operation to build the snapshot.

_Scan or refresh finished_ (news).
A scan or a refresh whose conclusion is finished (ADR 0027) makes one for every ServerAdmin, when at least one of its figures is above zero.
A stopped or failed scan or refresh makes none, and so does one that changed nothing, so a daily scan of a quiet library says nothing.
The end of a scan is nobody's action, so the ServerAdmin who started the scan is told too.
The figures include the titles it added and removed and the files it skipped; the decision that builds the kind fixes the exact figures.
A later such scan or refresh of the same library adds its figures to the unread Notification's.
The line therefore counts everything since the ServerAdmin last read it, and a smaller scan overwrites nothing.

_Unmatched items_ (news).
A finished scan that added items it could not match makes one for every ServerAdmin.
Its snapshot holds how many unmatched items the library held when the scan finished, as in "Movies: 14 titles need matching".
Fixing a match changes nothing stored: the line says what the ServerAdmin was told after the scan, and the library's `unmatched` filter counts the items now.
A later scan that adds unmatched items replaces the number in the unread Notification and moves it to the top.
A scan that adds none changes nothing, so a ServerAdmin who keeps some items unmatched on purpose is not told again.
There is one per library, never one per item.

*Why unmatched items are news, not a request:* an unmatched item can be the right state, such as a file that no provider knows.
Nobody waits for it, and no single decision settles a library's unmatched items.
As a request, the Notification would never resolve, or it would ask again after every scan.

=== The GraphQL contract

One interface fits every kind, and a second interface marks the requests, in the same way that `InputMutationError` extends `MutationError` (ADR 0026).
Each kind that the server sends has its own concrete type, which holds the kind's snapshot and its subject's id.

[source,graphql]
----
"""
What the signed-in Account was told when something happened to a subject (ADR 0063). The
concrete type names the kind and holds what the Account was told and the subject's id.
"""
interface Notification {
    id: ID!
    """
    The Notification's line, in English, for a client that shows this type but handles it no
    further. A client may word its own line from the typed fields. Never parse this one.
    """
    message: String!
    "When the first event made the Notification, as an ISO-8601 instant."
    createdAt: String!
    "When its latest event happened, as an ISO-8601 instant. The list orders by it, newest first."
    latestEventAt: String!
    """
    True until the recipient marks the Notification read. A request is also read once it
    resolves, and it never becomes unread again.
    """
    unread: Boolean!
}

"""
A Notification that waits for a decision on its subject. Its state is stored on it, and the
subject's events write it. Once expiresAt has passed, an unresolved request reads as EXPIRED.
A decision or a withdrawal made before expiresAt that arrives after it still replaces EXPIRED,
and the change is pushed.
"""
interface RequestNotification implements Notification {
    id: ID!
    message: String!
    createdAt: String!
    latestEventAt: String!
    unread: Boolean!
    "When the subject expires unless someone decides it first. Null when the subject has no expiry."
    expiresAt: String
    "How the request resolved. Null while it waits."
    resolution: RequestResolution
    "When the request resolved, as an ISO-8601 instant; expiresAt for an expiry. Null while it waits."
    resolvedAt: String
}

enum RequestResolution {
    "Someone with the authority accepted, rejected or declined the subject."
    DECIDED
    "The proposer canceled the subject, a newer proposal replaced it, or the server invalidated it."
    WITHDRAWN
    "The subject's expiry passed with no decision."
    EXPIRED
}

* `NotificationIdCountOutOfRangeError` and `NotificationRetentionOutOfRangeError` join the shared error types (ADR 0026).
  `NotificationIdCountOutOfRangeError` is an `InputMutationError`: `markNotificationsRead` returns it, and marks nothing, when it receives no id or more than 100.
  An id that the server cannot decode is an `InvalidIdError` at that id's `inputPath`, and the call marks nothing.
  `markNotificationsRead` has no not-found error: an id that does not exist and another Account's Notification are skipped alike, so its answer tells the caller nothing about another Account's Notifications.
* A concrete type carries its subject's id, never the subject.
  `shareId` is the id of a `ProfileShare`, in the same encoding as `ProfileShare.id`, so a client opens the share through the share queries and its cache meets the share under its own identity.
  The share queries may answer that the share does not exist, and a client then shows the Notification's own line and nothing more.
* ADR 0042 requires a selected Profile for every subscription field except Library status, and the active streams of ADR 0013 need a ServerAdmin.
  This decision amends ADR 0042: `notificationsChanged` needs an Account session that is not device-bound, with or without a selected Profile, because Notifications belong to their recipient Account.
  ADR 0042 also records the other such exception, the share changes of streamarr-server#871.
* Every concrete type exists only for a kind that the server sends.
  The three sharing types are the first build.
  The three Profile management types join the schema with Profile management.
  The library kinds are specified only, live in this ADR alone, and have no type in the schema.
* A new concrete type is an addition to the schema, but a deployed client cannot match a type that its generated `possibleTypes` lack.
  As for union members in ADR 0026, clients that know a new kind ship before the server sends it.
* A Notification keeps its id and its concrete type for its whole life, because only an event of its own kind updates it in place.
  A client's normalized cache therefore updates the one entry, and the changed edge moves it in the list.
* The list's order and paging are the server's shared ones (see <<order-and-paging>>).
  An update in place moves a Notification to the top of the list, as a newer last watched moves an item in Continue Watching.
* Clients keep the list in their normalized cache (link:0048-clients-keep-live-lists-in-their-normalized-cache.adoc[ADR 0048]).
  The list is one list per Account; a Profile or Household switch resets the client's store as ADR 0042 requires, and the client reads the list again.

[[live-changes]]
=== Live changes

`notificationsChanged` pushes each committed change to a recipient's Notifications:

* a Notification is made or updated in place;
* a request resolves;
* a recipient marks Notifications read: a call that changes at least one row pushes one change, with each row that it marked, and a call that changes nothing pushes none;
* Notifications are dismissed: each recipient that lost at least one receives one change, with a null edge for each.

The module pushes after commit, from the rows that the transaction wrote, and a transaction that rolls back pushes nothing.

Every write of a share goes through `ProfileShareLifecycle`, which publishes a `ProfileShareChangedEvent` for each share that the write changes.
The identity domain hears that event inside the transaction and publishes the Notifications to make and the requests to resolve, and the live updates of shares hear it too.
These names come from the work for streamarr-server#871.
The event covers the operations on an offer and a share themselves.
It also covers the operations that change shares on their way, such as connecting a Profile to an Account, disabling an Account, revoking ServerAdmin authority, deleting or transferring an Account or a Profile, and relinquishing management.

It covers expiry too.
`ProfileShareExpirySweeper` runs once a minute by default.
It stores `EXPIRED` on each pending offer whose expiry time has passed, through `ProfileShareLifecycle`, so each expiry arrives as the same event as every other change.
The identity domain then resolves the offer's requests as expired, and `notificationsChanged` announces it like any other resolution.
The request already reads as expired from its stored expiry, so the sweep changes only when an open client learns of it.
A later request kind brings the same rule for its own subject, its expiry included.

Two deletions are not pushed:

* A deletion by retention, which deletes only history and changes no unread count.
* A deletion with its recipient Account.

An open list drops such Notifications when it is read again.

=== How the server builds it from what it has

The first build adds the module's own table and events, and no other mechanism.
It uses the parts that the server already has:

* *The module's inputs:* four kinds of application event (ADR 0010), which any domain may publish inside its transaction, and which the module hears inside that transaction:
** _tell_, with a kind, a subject reference, the recipients, the snapshot and the event's time, and for a request its subject's expiry, which every request kind built now gives;
** _resolve_, with a request kind, a subject reference, the resolution, the answer when the subject was decided, and the instant of the subject's change;
** _dismiss_, with a selector (see <<dismissal>>);
** _recipient deleted_, with the Account that its domain deletes.
* *Where they live:* the four events, and one record type for each kind's snapshot, live in the shared events package below the domains, as ADR 0010 places the library events.
  The domains depend on them, and the module depends on no domain.
  Unlike ADR 0010's library events, which carry only ids and are heard after commit, these carry the snapshot and are heard inside the publisher's transaction.
  The snapshot is what the module may not read for itself, and a Notification must commit or roll back with its event.
* *Publishing:* each domain turns its own events into the module's.
  For shares, the identity domain hears `ProfileShareChangedEvent` inside the transaction, works out the recipients from the relationship rows that it holds, leaves out the Account that acted, builds the snapshot, and publishes _tell_, with the offer's expiry for its requests, and _resolve_, with the instant that the change recorded on the share.
  The identity domain publishes _dismiss_ when a HouseholdAdmin loses the role or moves to another Household, and when it deletes a share, a Profile or a Household, and it publishes _recipient deleted_ when it deletes an Account.
  For the library kinds later, the library domain does the same from `ScanEndedEvent` and `RefreshEndedEvent`, with the library operation's id added, and publishes _dismiss_ when it removes a library.
* *Storage:* one table that the module owns.
  Fixed columns hold everything that the module selects, orders, counts or coalesces by: the recipient's type and id, the kind, the form, the subject's type and id, the times, the read time, and for a request its state, its resolution time and its subject's expiry.
  The recipient and the subject are references, each a type and an id, with no foreign key.
  A kind with a new subject type therefore adds no column that points at another module's table, and the module learns of a deleted subject or recipient from events, never from the database.
  The snapshot is one structured column, a `jsonb` document that the kind's record type writes and reads.
  The unique indexes, the list, the count, the marks, the dismissals and retention never look inside it.
  A request's answer joins its snapshot when _resolve_ writes it, because what an answer can be depends on the kind.
* *Why one structured column, not typed columns:* the module never selects, orders or counts by a fact of a snapshot, so typed columns would buy no query.
  They would add a migration with every kind, and columns that are null on every other kind's rows.
  With one structured column, a new kind brings a name, a form, a subject type and a record type for its snapshot, and the module's shape does not change.
  A test writes and reads the snapshot of every kind, so a document that its record type cannot read fails the build, not a page.
* *Writing:* _tell_ applies the noise rule to news with one `INSERT … ON CONFLICT … DO UPDATE` against the unique index, and makes requests with `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` against the request index.
  A request's expiry column takes the expiry that _tell_ carries, and the module asks no other domain for it.
  _Scan or refresh finished_ adds its figures in the same statement, and the decision that builds it fixes how.
  _resolve_ updates the requests of its kind about its subject that no event has resolved yet: it writes the resolution and its instant where that instant is before the request's stored expiry, and expired, at the stored expiry, where it is not.
  _dismiss_ deletes what its selector selects.
  Both return the rows that they changed, for delivery.
* *Delivery:* after commit, a listener passes each change to the recipient's open operations through a `KeyedSubscriptionBroadcaster` keyed by Account id, as `watchStateChanged` is keyed by Profile id (ADR 0042).
* *Paging:* the server's shared pagination of ADR 0014, as <<order-and-paging>> sets out: a `PagedList` under `Pager`, read through `Keyset`, with `PositionCursorCodec` cursors, `OrderedEdge` edges and `PagedListContract`.
* *Authorization:* Cedar decides each read, mark and subscription through actions of the module's own, which the device-bound guardrail forbids, and it decides the retention setting through ServerAdmin authority.
  It is never asked about a share, an invitation, a Profile or a library.
  Every read selects the caller's own rows by recipient, and nothing else decides what it returns.
* *Payloads:* the sealed service outcomes and the shared payload helpers of ADR 0026.
* *Retention:* a sweep on a fixed schedule under the system actor, like the sweeps that expire share offers and delete old credential attempts.
  Its setting is shaped like the idle segment retention setting and kept in the same server settings, which the module reads through their own service.
  The sweep deletes a batch at a time, and each delete states the conditions of history and age in its `WHERE` (see <<retention>>).

=== Later channels

==== Email

Email attaches as a second channel for the same Notifications.
It needs a per-Account choice of the kinds to email, and a record for each Notification of what the channel sent.
It reads the same line and link target that every client reads, and it sends to the Account's email address.
An update in place is a new event, and the email channel decides whether to send again or to send a digest.
The Notification, its kinds, its recipients and its read state stay as they are.

==== The TV apps

A Notification stays addressed to its recipient, and nothing in it names a session or a client.
The TV apps need a decision of their own: whose Notifications a shared TV may show, which kinds suit a shared screen, and whether a Profile becomes a recipient.
For example, a TV could show only the Notifications of the Account whose Personal Profile is selected there.
A device-bound session may not administer (ADR 0024), so a TV could show news but never decide a request.
That decision narrows the guardrail for device-bound sessions, and it adds no field to a Notification.

=== Tests

Implementation starts with failing tests (link:0005-tdd-and-behavior-testing.adoc[ADR 0005]).
The tests show that:

* each recipient gets one copy when the event happens, the Account that made an offer is on its offering side, and the Account that acts gets none;
* an Account on both sides of a share that a ServerAdmin ends receives one _Share ended_ news;
* an Account that offered a Profile and stopped managing it before the share ended, and is not a ServerAdmin, gets no _Share ended_ news;
* a recipient keeps its news, with the Profile's name that it was told, after it loses the relationship through which it was told: a HouseholdAdmin of a share's target Household, told that a ProfileManager ended the share, still reads that news after it is demoted, and the share query for the same share answers not found;
* a rename of the Profile after the event changes no stored Notification, for either side;
* a HouseholdAdmin who is demoted, or whose Account moves to another Household, loses its waiting requests about offers into that Household from the list, the count and a live change; it keeps its resolved requests and its news, and it gets no request back when it is promoted again;
* an offer and a demotion of one of the target Household's HouseholdAdmins that run at the same moment leave that Account no waiting request, whichever commits first;
* a person who gains a role later gets no Notification for what was already waiting;
* a request reads as decided with its answer, withdrawn or expired as the subject's events write its state, for every recipient, and it reads as expired from the expiry that _tell_ carried before the sweep stores the offer's expiry;
* an acceptance whose instant is before the offer's expiry, and whose resolution reaches the module after that expiry, resolves the requests as decided, with the answer and the acceptance's instant, and pushes the change; a withdrawal whose instant is at or after the expiry leaves them expired, at the expiry;
* each path that changes a share, the expiry sweep included, writes the requests' state and pushes a change that carries the edge and the unread count that a fresh query returns;
* a recipient keeps one unread news per kind and subject, also under two events at the same moment; news of another kind about the same subject makes a Notification of its own, a request never updates in place, and a repeated _tell_ makes no second request;
* when an Account's management of a Profile is removed, granted again and removed again before the Account reads the first removal, the Account has one unread Notification, with the second removal's time and the Profile's name at that removal; after the Account reads it, a further removal makes a new one;
* two invitations to manage one Profile, sent to two addresses that one Account has had, both wait with a request each; when the Account accepts one, the other's request resolves as withdrawn, and neither was withdrawn before;
* an invitation that nobody can accept makes no request;
* a dismissal by id and a dismissal by subject, each narrowed to a recipient, to kinds and to a form, delete exactly what they select, push one change for each recipient that lost a Notification, and roll back with the transaction of their event;
* deleting a share, a Profile or a Household dismisses every Notification about it, for every recipient, and deleting a Profile also dismisses the Notifications about its invitations, which keep their rows;
* deleting a recipient Account deletes its Notifications and no other Account's;
* the list passes the shared `PagedListContract`, newest `latestEventAt` first, and a page that continues from a Notification that has since moved to the top, or been dismissed, starts where that Notification was;
* `markNotificationsRead` marks only the caller's own unread Notifications among the ids it receives, and skips an id that does not exist, another Account's and a read one alike, with no error;
* a Notification whose transaction commits after the page was read stays unread when the page's ids are marked, even when its time sorts below the rows that the page showed;
* a call to `markNotificationsRead` that changes at least one row pushes one live change, and a call that changes nothing pushes none;
* retention deletes read news and resolved requests older than the period, a request that reads as expired from its stored expiry included, and keeps unread news and waiting requests, a disabled recipient's included;
* a device-bound session gets `FORBIDDEN` from every Notification read, mark and subscription;
* the module's code depends on no other domain's package, only on the shared events package and the server's shared parts, and it reads no table that it does not own;
* every kind's snapshot is written and read back unchanged;
* the two library kinds, defined only in test code, fit the general model: the same writer, noise rule, dismissal and retention handle them, with _Scan or refresh finished_ adding its figures.

=== Explicitly deferred

* Email, and every other channel outside the web client.
* The TV apps, any Notification on a device-bound session, and a Profile as a recipient.
* A per-Account choice of the kinds to receive, or later to email.
* Marking a Notification unread, and dismissing a Notification by hand.
* News when an offer or an invitation expires or the server withdraws it.
* A Notification when a scan or a refresh fails or is stopped.
* A Notification when a viewer shares a playback report (ADR 0013).
  It would be news for every ServerAdmin, with the Shared reports list as its subject.
* A push to a browser or a phone while no Streamarr page is open.
* Building the two library kinds.

== Consequences

* A person learns about an offer, its answer or the end of a share without opening the sharing pages, and sees them in one list.
* The sharing pages still list and count from the shares, and Notifications change nothing that those pages show.
* The first build follows streamarr-server#871, for `ProfileShareLifecycle` and `ProfileShareChangedEvent`, and streamarr-server#870, so that every ordinary offer answers a share code that the target Household issued.
* Every write of a share goes through `ProfileShareLifecycle`, because a write that bypasses it publishes no `ProfileShareChangedEvent`, tells nobody, resolves no request and leaves open screens stale.
  A request whose resolution is missed reads as waiting until its stored expiry, and never longer.
* Each publishing domain owns who is told, what they are told, how its requests resolve and when its Notifications are dismissed.
  A domain that changes a relationship through which requests reach people must dismiss them in the same transaction; one that forgets leaves a request in a list until it resolves or expires.
* Listing, counting, marking and pushing read the caller's own rows and make no authorization decision for each Notification.
  The unread count is one indexed count, however many relationships the caller has lost.
* A Notification shows what was said, not what is true now: a renamed Profile keeps its old name in older Notifications, a resolved request keeps how it resolved, and an _Unmatched items_ line keeps the number from its scan.
* A recipient keeps its news after it loses the relationship through which it was told.
  The Household's side keeps a Profile's name after the offer or the share is over, and a former ProfileManager keeps the Profile's name in its news, until retention deletes the Notification or the Profile's deletion dismisses it; ADR 0024 records the Household's side's exception.
* A line names the Profile alone.
  A client that wants the Household's name or the offerer's name opens the share through the share queries, which may answer that it does not exist.
* The events that the module hears carry snapshots and are heard inside the publisher's transaction, unlike ADR 0010's library events, which carry ids and are heard after commit.
* A dismissal deletes, and each recipient that loses a Notification receives a live change, so its open lists and its count follow without a read.
* An expired request leaves an open client's unread count when the sweep stores its offer's expiry, within about a minute of the expiry time.
  A read during that minute already leaves it out.
* An update in place keeps a Notification's id, so marking the ids that a page showed also marks an update in place that the page had not shown yet.
* Deleting a Profile, a Household or a library sends no news about it, and it dismisses what was said about it.
* An accepted share that later ends leaves its offering side two lines, one for the answer and one for the end, because different kinds of news about one subject sit side by side.
* A Notification never changes its kind or its concrete type.
* Each new kind adds a concrete type, which clients learn before the server sends it, and no column and no migration.
* The list adds no paging of its own, so it keeps every rule of the shared pagination, and a change to those rules reaches it with every other list.
* The server gains a retention setting and a daily task.
* `notificationsChanged`, like the share changes of streamarr-server#871, is a subscription that every Account may open without a selected Profile, and ADR 0042 records both exceptions.

== Rejected Alternatives

* Authorize a Notification at read time from the reader's live relationships — one rule would be stated three times, in a Cedar policy, in a SQL filter for the list and the count, and in a resolver for the subject, and the module would read the subject through a second path, all to take back what the reader was already told.
* Store on each Notification the relationship or the side through which it reached its recipient — the module would keep a copy of another module's relationships and answer for their changes, while the domain that owns a relationship already knows which waiting requests its change ends.
* A separate notification service behind a message broker — Streamarr runs one server on one database (ADR 0027), and events inside the publisher's transaction give the same decoupling with no delivery guarantees to manage.
* Read a request's state from its subject at each read — the module would read another module's tables and apply the subject's read rule again; the subject's domain already publishes every change, and the stored expiry bounds a missed one.
* Resolve as expired every request whose stored expiry has passed when its resolution reaches the module — an acceptance made in time whose transaction waited for locks would leave a request that reads as expired beside the share that it made active.
* Find a request's expiry from its subject or from the lifetime setting — the module would read another domain, and the setting applies only to subjects made after it changes, so _tell_ carries the expiry.
* Keep a dismissed Notification in a third state — every list, count and mark would have to leave it out, and nothing needs it back.
* Dismiss a person's news when it loses the relationship through which it was told — the news was true, and its recipient could know it when it was told; only a request asks something of its recipient.
* Let the module work out a dismissal from a reason, such as a lost role — the module would have to know each domain's relationships, while a selector by id or by subject keeps the reason with the domain that owns it.
* Resolve the subject inside a Notification, as a `share` field or any field like it — the field would need a read rule of its own beside the subject's, and the subject's id already opens it through the ordinary queries.
* Typed columns for each kind's facts, or a column for each subject type — each new kind would add a migration and columns that are null on every other kind's rows, and a subject column would point at another module's table.
* Address a group, such as a Household's HouseholdAdmins, and check membership when a member reads — each member still needs a read state of its own, an Account that joins later would inherit what was said before, and every list and count would read each group's membership.
* Count waiting things from unread requests — a person who gains a role later would see no count, and the count would fall when a reader marks a request read or retention deletes it.
* Write a read time when a request resolves — a resolved request already counts as read from its state, and a read time would claim that its recipient had read it.
* Keep a request unread for each recipient until that recipient answers — the other recipients would see a dot for something already settled.
* Key "Offer waiting" on the Profile and the Household, and update it when an offer is replaced — the offer is what waits and what a HouseholdAdmin decides, and share codes already make each new offer one that the Household asked for.
* One unread Notification per subject across all kinds — it would replace a step of what happened with the next one: an unread "Offer answered" would become "Share ended", and the Account would never learn that the share was accepted.
* A new Notification for every event, with no update in place — a daily scan would leave one line a day for each library.
* Replace the stored figures when a later scan updates a scan's Notification — a smaller scan would overwrite the figures of a larger one that the ServerAdmin had not read yet.
* One Notification for each unmatched item — the first scan of a new library could leave hundreds of lines.
* Read the library's current unmatched count into the line — the module would read the library's items, and the line would stop being what the ServerAdmin was told; the library's `unmatched` filter counts the items now.
* Unmatched items as a request — an unmatched item can be correct, nobody waits for it, and the request would never resolve or would ask again after every scan.
* Copy the offerer's, the Household's or the inviter's name into the snapshot — each would outlive what ADR 0024 lets its recipient see of it.
* Read the Profile's current name for a reader who may still see the Profile — a line would change after it was read, and the module would read the Profile.
* Keep a Notification after its subject is deleted — it would keep a deleted Profile's name, which ADR 0024 lets stay nowhere, and it has nothing to link to.
* Let retention delete unread news — news stays until it is read, and the noise rule already keeps unread news few.
* Push an offer's expiry at the very moment its time runs out — it would need a timer for each offer, while the request already reads as expired from its stored expiry and the sweep pushes within about a minute.
* Mark Notifications read when the list opens — the dot would clear for lines that the reader never looked at.
* Mark read every unread Notification — it would mark read a Notification that arrived while the reader looked at the list.
* Mark read every Notification at or below a place in the list — a Notification takes its time inside the transaction that changes its subject, so one that commits after the page was read can sort among the rows that the page showed and be marked read although the reader never saw it.
* A second mutation that marks one Notification — the same mutation marks one id or many.
* A link in the Notification — the server would depend on one client's routes.
* One Notification type with a kind enum and every kind's fields nullable — no type would say what its kind carries; concrete types do, as ADR 0026's error members do.
* Use the live share updates of streamarr-server#871, or another subscription, as the store — a subscription stores nothing and reaches only open screens.
* Notifications on device-bound sessions now — a shared TV would show one Account's Notifications to everyone who uses it.
